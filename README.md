# Particle Accelerator

A complete, playable particle accelerator for **Minecraft 1.21.1 (Fabric, Java 21)**.

You mine lead, craft an accelerator blueprint, build a ring out of beam pipes, bending and
focusing magnets, RF cavities, vacuum hardware and detectors, pump it down, cool the magnets,
inject a beam, ramp it to energy, collide it — and find out which of the 118 elements you can make.

Nothing in this mod is faked:

* the beam is a real relativistic simulation (Lorentz force, momentum in MeV/c, β and γ, synchrotron
  radiation, betatron motion through a one-turn map, gas scattering, instabilities),
* the magnets, cavities, vacuum system, cooling and cryogenics each move real numbers,
* the blueprint reads the blocks you actually placed and reports the exact errors, with coordinates,
* the collision products come from the real branching of the interaction and are recorded in the
  detector readout and the element collection,
* every element of the periodic table has its isotopes, and radioactive nuclides decay with their
  real half lives (compressed for play).

## Building it

```
./gradlew build          # needs JDK 21 (the mod target); the Gradle daemon itself wants JDK 25
```

The mod jar lands in `build/libs/`. GitHub Actions builds it on every push
(`.github/workflows/build.yml`) and uploads the jar as a downloadable artifact.

Install the jar together with **Fabric Loader** and **Fabric API** in `mods/`.

## Playing it

1. **Mine lead** (`lead_ore` / `deepslate_lead_ore`, added to every overworld biome) and the usual
   copper, iron, redstone and netherite.
2. **Craft the blueprint** (lead ingot + paper) and the **research journal** (book, ink sac, boron
   powder). The journal shows the eight chapters, and the two tools together are your HUD.
3. Stand where the centre of the machine should be, hold the blueprint and right click: the guide
   opens. Deploy it and ghost blocks appear over everything that still has to be built.
4. Follow the ghosts — they are generated by the server from the real design, colour-coded per
   subsystem; the **Construction** page lists what is missing, where, and why.
5. Shield the tunnel (concrete, lead, water, borated polyethylene), put down power supplies,
   cooling units and cryogenics, and place the control computer next to the ring.
6. Open the **control computer** and work the machine: *Start/Stop*, *Inject*, the magnet and RF
   channels, the energy presets (1 MeV → 100 TeV), the vacuum, cooling and cryogenics readouts, the
   collision rate, luminosity and dose rate, and 3-minute strip charts of energy, rate and pressure.
7. Open a **detector** to read particle identification (energy, momentum, charge, mass, velocity,
   lifetime), the event rate, multiplicity and the last event.
8. Open the **journal** for the chapter progression, the experiment log and the 118-element
   collection.

### The physics, in one paragraph

A beam particle carries position, velocity, mass, charge, energy, momentum, direction and lifetime.
It is tracked through a lattice built from the blocks you placed: dipoles bend by
`θ = L·B/(Bρ)`, quadrupoles focus with `k = g/(Bρ)` in one plane and defocus in the other (rotate one
and it swaps), sextupoles correct chromaticity, cavities add `ΔE = qV·sin(φ)` at the synchronous
frequency, and the vacuum determines gas scattering and beam lifetime. The energy ramp is limited by
the RF voltage needed to overcome the synchrotron radiation loss per turn, which grows with the
fourth power of the energy per unit mass — the reason 100 TeV needs either a really large ring or
superconducting magnets, and why the power bill scales the way it does.

## Project layout

```
src/main/java/com/particlephysics/
  physics/      units, relativity, optics, tracking, vacuum, decays, collisions, the beam
  elements/     all 118 elements, 532 isotopes, decay chains, nuclear reactions
  accelerator/  machine catalogue, design/blueprint, the runtime of a machine, the control room
  block/ blockentity/ registry/   blocks, block entities, items, ore generation
  radiation/ world/               shielding, dose, persistent save data (research, radiation, sites)
  net/ command/                   the protocol between client and server, and /accelerator
  client/                         screens (blueprint, computer, detector, journal, machine),
                                  HUD and world rendering (ghosts, beam)
  config/                         everything tunable in config/particle-accelerator.json
tools/  gen_element_data.py       regenerates the element/isotope tables
        gen_assets.py             regenerates textures, models, blockstates, recipes, loot, tags
        syntax_check.py           tree-sitter syntax check for the whole source tree
```

## Commands

`/accelerator status | deploy <radius> | start | stop | inject | field <T> | energy <MeV> |
 rf <MV> | species <name> | elements | radiation | config [key value] | save`

## Configuration

`config/particle-accelerator.json` (also editable in game with `/accelerator config`): physics
accuracy, simulation quality (`LOW`/`MEDIUM`/`HIGH`/`EXTREME`), ticks per second, maximum particles
per machine, energy/power/radiation/element-production scaling, half-life compression, vacuum,
instability and synchrotron radiation switches, rendering switches and autosave interval.

## Data

Everything the player earns is stored per world in `<world>/particleaccelerator/`: discovered
elements and isotopes, quest progress, experiment log, dose, accelerator sites and their setpoints,
and the radiation inventory. It survives restarts and is written on shutdown and on a timer.
